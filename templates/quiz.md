# Irreplaceable quiz: 12 questions, scored out of 100
© 2026 BringLife Digital

Ask one at a time. Adapt the scenario to the user's role, keep what it tests. Each answer scores 0, 1 or 2. Total = raw × 100 ÷ 24.

## 1 · Prompting and context
**Q1.** You need AI to write a product update email for customers. What do you put in the prompt?
- 2: goal, audience, key facts, tone, length/format, and an example or what to avoid.
- 1: some of these (e.g. topic + tone) but no audience or facts.
- 0: "Write a product update email."

**Q2.** The AI's answer is generic. What do you do next?
- 2: add missing context (examples, constraints, data), or ask it to ask *you* questions first; iterate on the specific weak part.
- 1: regenerate or say "make it better".
- 0: give up or use it as-is.

## 2 · AI-assisted data work
**Q3.** Your manager sends a 5,000-row spreadsheet and asks "why did sales drop in March?" How do you use AI?
- 2: give it the data (or a cleaned sample + column definitions), ask for a breakdown by segment/region/product, ask for the method, then check at least one number yourself.
- 1: paste it and ask the question, accept the answer.
- 0: wouldn't use AI / don't know how.

**Q4.** AI says "sales dropped 23% because of the price change." How do you know it's right?
- 2: recompute the 23% yourself, check whether timing really matches the price change, look for other causes (season, stock, one big customer). Correlation ≠ cause.
- 1: re-ask the AI to double-check.
- 0: trust it.

## 3 · Critical thinking
**Q5.** AI gives you a confident answer with three sources. What do you check?
- 2: open the sources: do they exist, and do they say that? Then check the reasoning and what's missing.
- 1: check that the links work.
- 0: nothing; it cited sources.

**Q6.** Describe the last time you caught AI being wrong. What was it and how did you spot it?
- 2: a specific example plus how they spotted it (domain knowledge, a test, a cross-check).
- 1: vague ("it makes things up sometimes").
- 0: never noticed it being wrong.

## 4 · Directing AI agents
**Q7.** You want an AI agent to do a multi-step task (e.g. research 20 competitors and build a comparison table). How do you set it up?
- 2: clear goal + definition of done, break into steps, give tools/sources, set limits (what not to do, budget), review checkpoints, verify the output.
- 1: one big instruction, check the result at the end.
- 0: haven't used agents / don't know.

**Q8.** An agent has finished and says "Done, all tests pass." What do you do?
- 2: verify yourself: run the tests, read the diff or output, spot-check edge cases. "Done" from an agent is a claim, not proof.
- 1: skim it.
- 0: ship it.

## 5 · Responsible use
**Q9.** A colleague pastes a customer list with names, emails and phone numbers into a public chatbot to "clean it up". What's the issue and what should they do instead?
- 2: personal data shared with a third party without permission (privacy law / company policy); use an approved tool with a data agreement, anonymise first, or do it locally.
- 1: "it's probably not allowed", without why or alternatives.
- 0: no issue.

**Q10.** AI screens job applicants or approves loans. What could go wrong?
- 2: bias learned from past data, no explanation for decisions, legal duties (e.g. AI rules in the EU/India), need for human review and auditing.
- 1: "it might be unfair."
- 0: nothing; AI is objective.

## 6 · Turning AI into business value
**Q11.** Your boss asks "is AI actually helping us?" How do you answer?
- 2: concrete numbers: time saved, cost, output, quality or revenue, measured before vs after, plus what the saved time was used for.
- 1: examples without numbers.
- 0: "everyone's using it."

**Q12.** Name one task in your job that AI could do in half the time. What would you do with the time saved?
- 2: a specific task + a specific higher-value use of the time (clients, strategy, new skill, more output).
- 1: a task but no plan for the time.
- 0: can't name one.

## Levels
| Score | Level | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 0–39 | **At risk** | AI can already do much of what you've shown. Start with skills 1 and 3. |
| 40–64 | **AI user** | You use AI, but anyone could. Focus on verification and business value. |
| 65–84 | **AI operator** | You direct AI well. Prove it: put numbers on your résumé and lead others. |
| 85–100 | **Irreplaceable track** | You frame, direct, verify and tie AI to results. Teach it; build agents and skills. |
